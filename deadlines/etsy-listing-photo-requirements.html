What do Etsy sellers need to know about the unedited listing photo rule?

Etsy Help says that for most items, each listing needs at least one unedited photo of the actual item against a real background. On that photo you may crop, resize, add a watermark, or change the file format. You may not otherwise edit it, including the lighting. Other photos in the same listing can be edited, including with AI, if they still show what the buyer will receive. These two Etsy Help pages do not name a single last day for the rule.

What to check on a listing

  1. Confirm the listing has at least one photo of the actual item against a real background that has not been edited, except for a crop, a resize, a watermark, or a file-format change. Etsy says one unedited photo covers the listing even when the listing has variations.
  2. Leave the lighting alone on that unedited photo. Etsy says the unedited photo should show the item against a real background without lighting changes, because some editing tools use AI and it is not always obvious.
  3. On the other photos, editing is allowed, including AI, for lighting, color, crop, resize, or background. Etsy says those edits still have to show what the buyer will receive. Do not hide flaws, conceal damage, or make the item look different.
  4. The first image does not have to be the unedited photo. Etsy says it should still be the clearest, most realistic picture of the item, because it is the first image buyers see.
  5. If any listing photo was edited with AI, Etsy says to note that in the listing description.
  6. Do not count a video as the unedited photo. Etsy says videos do not meet that requirement.
  7. Use a file type Etsy lists: .jpg, .gif, .png, .svg, or .heic. Etsy says animated .gif files and transparent .png files are not supported, and that transparent areas appear black.
  8. Etsy recommends listing photos of at least 2000 pixels in width and height. Etsy says the first listing photo should be at least 635 pixels in width and height so it does not show up lower in search. Etsy also says images larger than 1MB may not finish uploading.
  9. Every image, edited or not, has to reflect the item's true color, condition, scale, and materials. Etsy says images cannot create false expectations about what the buyer will receive.

Item types Etsy calls out

  • Print-on-demand: if a production partner prints your original design on a standard base product, such as a t-shirt or mug, Etsy says you may use a stock-photo mockup when the photo's owner has authorized that use.
  • Digital downloads: if the item has a visual component, such as a printable or template, include a preview of the actual content. If it has no visual component, Etsy says you may use representative imagery, including AI-generated images, as long as it does not mislead the buyer.
  • Customized or made-to-order items you make yourself: Etsy says to use photos of real finished items you have made before.
  • Craft patterns, recipes, seeds, or similar items: Etsy says to include at least one real unedited photo of something made with the product. AI-generated images cannot be the only images of that finished result. The image-requirements article also says functional guides such as patterns, recipes, or instructions need a photo of an actual result, not an AI-generated or simulated result.
  • Etsy says some exceptions depend on what you sell, and that listing images of minors have to follow Etsy's separate policy. This page does not restate that minors policy.

Etsy says that if AI-generated images are detected while you create a listing, Etsy will show tips to help you represent the item. Etsy also says that, in the beginning, reports of possible AI misuse help Etsy understand how buyers see those images and will not result in immediate enforcement action.

FAQ

Does every Etsy listing photo have to be unedited?

No. Etsy Help says that for most items you need at least one unedited photo of the actual item against a real background. Other listing photos can use photo-editing tools, including AI, as long as they do not misrepresent what the buyer will receive. Your first listing image does not have to be that unedited photo.

What can I change on the required unedited photo?

Etsy Help says you can crop or resize the photo, add a watermark, or change its file format. The photo should not be otherwise edited or changed. Etsy also says the unedited photo should show the item against a real background without changes to the lighting.

Can I use AI on Etsy listing photos?

Apart from the required unedited photo, Etsy Help says you can use AI-assisted tools to crop or resize, adjust lighting or color, or change backgrounds, as long as the item still looks like what the buyer will receive. Do not use AI to hide damage or change the item's appearance. If you use AI to edit any listing photo, Etsy says to note that in the listing description.

Does an unedited video count as the required photo?

No. Etsy Help says videos do not count toward the unedited-photo requirement. Etsy says photos give buyers a still view of the item.

What if I sell print-on-demand, digital, or made-to-order items?

Etsy Help says requirements can differ by item type. A print-on-demand seller whose production partner prints an original design on a standard base product may use a stock-photo mockup if the photo's owner has authorized that use. A digital item with a visual component needs a preview of the actual content. A digital item with no visual component may use representative imagery, including AI-generated images, if it does not mislead buyers. Customized items you make yourself should use photos of real finished items you have made. Patterns, recipes, seeds, or similar items need at least one real unedited photo of something made using the product, and AI-generated images cannot be the only images of that finished result.
