ChatGPT custom GPTs are scheduled to retire (Dec 11, 2026)

OpenAI says ChatGPT custom GPTs are scheduled to retire on Dec 11, 2026, and creators can migrate each GPT to a plugin when migration is available for their account. For affected Enterprise workspaces, creation of new custom GPTs is planned to end Oct 26, 2026. Custom actions, the selected model, sharing settings and unpublished edits do not carry over, so check those first.

Who it's for: anyone who created a custom GPT or relies on one for work, on any ChatGPT plan, including admins of Enterprise workspaces.

Last updated Oct 21, 2026. Current dates: Oct 26, 2026 (planned, affected Enterprise workspaces), Dec 11, 2026 (standard retirement), Feb 11, 2027 (only with an approved deferral). Dates are subject to change.

Plain-English summary

OpenAI says it is "planning to retire custom GPTs and help creators migrate them to plugins." The change affects all ChatGPT plans, but migration and plugin access may differ by account or workspace, so OpenAI says to follow your in-product notice. What counts is the account or workspace where the GPT was created.

Your GPT is not gone yet. OpenAI says existing GPTs remain usable until the retirement date, and your existing conversations with custom GPTs "will remain accessible after retirement." After you migrate a GPT, the original stays usable until retirement but becomes read-only.

Under OpenAI's planned migration workflow, a GPT's instructions become a skill in the new plugin, its connected apps are added as apps, and its knowledge files are copied into the plugin's reference files. Some things do not carry over: custom actions, the GPT's selected model, its sharing settings, and drafts or unpublished edits. The new plugin starts private.

Free steps, from OpenAI's FAQ

If you created the GPT

  1. List the GPTs you created and the ones your team relies on, and note which account or workspace each was created in.
  2. Review each GPT's instructions, reference files and connected apps. Flag any GPT that uses custom actions: OpenAI says they "do not transfer through the migration workflow", and rebuilding the connection may need an available app or a custom MCP server.
  3. Finish and publish any essential edits. OpenAI says "Migration uses the GPT's latest published version. Drafts and unpublished edits do not transfer." Publishing does not require sharing it publicly.
  4. Save a few familiar prompts, plus at least one harder case, and keep the GPT's current answers so you can compare.
  5. When migration is available for your account, go to My GPTs and select Migrate to plugin for the GPT, then follow the instructions to create the plugin.
  6. Run the same prompts on the plugin. OpenAI suggests checking that it selects the right skill and follows your instructions, uses the expected reference material, produces complete answers or files in the required format, and has the tools and integrations the workflow needs.
  7. Share the plugin with the people who need it (it starts private and migration does not give existing users access), and ask one of them to confirm they can install and use it.

If you only use someone else's GPT

OpenAI says you do not need to migrate a GPT you only use, and permission to use it does not let you migrate it yourself. Check the notice on the GPT and the creator's guidance about a replacement. Access to the original GPT does not automatically give you access to a replacement plugin.

Don't see Migrate to plugin?

OpenAI says migration may not be available to your account yet, or plugin access may be disabled. Check the notice in your account and make sure you are in the account or workspace where the GPT was created. In an Enterprise workspace, the GPT's creator or a workspace admin can migrate it when migration is available and plugins are enabled.
