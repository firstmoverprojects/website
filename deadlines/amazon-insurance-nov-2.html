Plain-English summary

Amazon already requires commercial liability insurance (at least $1 million per occurrence and in total) for sellers whose Amazon.com sales go over $10,000 in any month. Amazon's announcement says two changes start November 2, 2026. First, sellers with products in categories that have enhanced safety listing requirements need that coverage for those products regardless of the $10,000 threshold, for new and existing listings. Amazon names children's products, cosmetic and ingestible products, and lithium battery products, and says the list is not limited to those. Second, sellers based in Mainland China must get new policies through Amazon's Insurance Accelerator program. Check Amazon's own pages for which rules apply to your products.

Why did I receive the Nov 2, 2026 Commercial Liability Insurance email when my sales are under $10,000/month (even $1,500/month)?

Under Amazon's Nov 2, 2026 update (Policy G200386300), enhanced-safety product categories require $1,000,000 commercial general liability insurance regardless of monthly sales volume. Amazon's examples include children's products, cosmetic and ingestible products, and lithium battery products, and Amazon says that list is not complete. Sellers also see the notice on topicals, cosmetics, supplements, and other dietary or safety-regulated items, including lithium-battery electronics.

Even a single old or inactive listing in your Manage Inventory catalog can trigger the notice. That includes sellers whose sales are under $10,000 a month, and sellers around $1,500 a month. The $10,000 gross-proceeds rule is a separate, older trigger. It does not turn the enhanced-safety rule off.

Free decision rule: if the notice was triggered by an old discontinued or inactive ASIN you no longer sell, permanently deleting that listing from Manage Inventory (rather than just closing it) can remove the category trigger at $0 cost. If you actively sell in that category, you must upload a compliant ACORD 25 certificate of insurance. This page cannot see your catalog. Confirm the listing in Manage Inventory and the notice in Account Health before you delete anything.

How do individual / sole-proprietor sellers using an offline insurance broker pass Amazon's review without Amazon Insurance Accelerator?

Amazon's Insurance Accelerator requirement on Policy G200386300 applies to sellers based in Mainland China, unless Amazon agrees otherwise in writing. It is not a requirement that every individual seller buy the policy through Amazon. An individual or sole proprietor who is not in that Mainland China rule can ask an offline broker to issue the ACORD 25. Amazon's Business Insurance verification still decides whether the file is accepted. Use this checklist so the certificate matches the published rule before you upload it.

  1. The insured name matches the Seller Central Legal Entity page character for character. Except for a single-member LLC, Amazon says the insured name must match the legal entity name in Account Info. For a single-member LLC, Amazon also allows the insured name to match the trade name or DBA. A sole proprietor's trade name is not that exception.
  2. The certificate names "Amazon.com Services LLC and its affiliates and assignees" as additional insured. Certificate holder alone is not the same thing.
  3. Limits are at least $1,000,000 per occurrence and $1,000,000 aggregate, covering product liability, bodily injury, and personal injury. Amazon's published line says the policy must cover liabilities from your business operations, including products, products/completed operations, and bodily injury, at USD 1 million per occurrence and in aggregate.
  4. The deductible is no greater than $10,000, and that amount is listed on the certificate. A $25,000 deductible does not meet this line.
  5. The policy covers all sales from products listed on Amazon.com.
  6. The policy has no sunset clause. A certificate often does not show this, so ask the broker to confirm it in writing.
  7. The insurer gives Amazon at least 30 days' notice of cancellation, modification, or non-renewal.

I sell products online. Amazon's announcement says that from November 2, 2026, sellers with products in categories with enhanced safety listing requirements (such as children's, cosmetic and ingestible, and lithium battery products) need commercial liability insurance with at least a $1 million limit regardless of sales volume. Please help me track this:
1. Make a checklist: does my product category fall under the requirement (I will check Amazon's own policy page)? Do I have a current policy? What is its limit, its dates and who is named on it? Who is my insurance contact?
2. List questions to ask an insurance agent, e.g. what documents Amazon asks for and how long it takes.
3. Remind me to read Amazon's own help pages for the exact rules and dates.
4. Set a reminder schedule: two weeks before, one week before, and three days before November 2.
Do not buy anything or submit anything. Only list what I should check and I will decide. Do not ask me for passwords and do not log in to any account.
