Before anything is uploaded to Seller Central, the pre-check spots two mismatches:

  1. Legal entity name. Seller Central says Jordan Lee. The certificate says Lee Home Goods. Amazon's G200386300 page says the insured name must match the legal entity in Account Info, except for a single-member LLC whose trade name Amazon allows. This sample seller is a sole proprietor, so the trade name is not that exception. The names do not match character for character.
  2. Deductible. The sample certificate lists $25,000. Amazon says the deductible must not be greater than $10,000 and that the amount must be listed on the certificate.

The kit's broker email script is the message this sample seller copies to an offline broker before submission. Sample script:

Subject: Please reissue the ACORD 25 before I upload it to Amazon

Please reissue my certificate with these corrections:
1. Named insured must read exactly: Jordan Lee
   Seller Central Legal Entity is Jordan Lee. The certificate shows Lee Home Goods.
   I am a sole proprietor, not a single-member LLC, so the trade name is not a substitute.
2. Name "Amazon.com Services LLC and its affiliates and assignees" as additional insured.
3. Show at least $1,000,000 each occurrence and $1,000,000 aggregate.
4. Replace the $25,000 deductible with $10,000 or less, and list that amount on the certificate.
5. Confirm in writing: no sunset clause, and the policy covers all sales on Amazon.com.
6. Give Amazon at least 30 days' notice of cancellation, modification, or non-renewal.

    Questions sellers are asking
    

    Why did I receive the Nov 2, 2026 Commercial Liability Insurance email when my sales are under $10,000/month (even $1,500/month)?
    

    Under Amazon's Nov 2, 2026 update (Policy G200386300), enhanced-safety product categories require $1,000,000 commercial general liability insurance regardless of monthly sales volume. Amazon's examples include children's products, cosmetic and ingestible products, and lithium battery products, and Amazon says that list is not complete. Sellers also see the notice on topicals, cosmetics, supplements, and other dietary or safety-regulated items. Even a single old or inactive listing in Manage Inventory can trigger the notice, including when sales are under $10,000 a month or about $1,500 a month.
    

    Free decision rule: if the notice was triggered by an old discontinued or inactive ASIN you no longer sell, permanently deleting that listing from Manage Inventory, rather than only closing it, can remove the category trigger at $0 cost. If you actively sell in that category, you must upload a compliant ACORD 25 certificate of insurance. This page cannot see your catalog. Confirm the listing in Manage Inventory and the notice in Account Health.
    

    How do individual / sole-proprietor sellers using an offline insurance broker pass Amazon's review without Amazon Insurance Accelerator?
    

    Amazon's Insurance Accelerator requirement on Policy G200386300 is for sellers based in Mainland China, unless Amazon agrees otherwise in writing. An individual or sole proprietor elsewhere can ask an offline broker for an ACORD 25 that meets this checklist:
    

      

    1. Insured name matches the Seller Central Legal Entity character for character. Amazon's exception is for a single-member LLC whose trade name Amazon allows, not for every sole proprietor using a DBA.
      2. 

    2. Additional insured reads "Amazon.com Services LLC and its affiliates and assignees".
      3. 

    3. At least $1,000,000 per occurrence and $1,000,000 aggregate, covering products, products/completed operations, and bodily injury. A commercial general liability form also addresses personal injury. The limit Amazon states is $1,000,000 per occurrence and in aggregate.
      4. 

    4. Deductible no greater than $10,000, and the amount is listed on the certificate.
      5. 

    5. The policy covers all sales on Amazon.com.
